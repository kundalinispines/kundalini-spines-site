Same concept, two things fixed. The columns no longer wait on each other, so there is no dead space at any length. And the centre is now a real spine that charges as you scroll — a continuous column of vertebrae, not a plain rule with dots on it. Switch treatments in the bar above; scroll to charge it.

What changed, and why the space was empty

V1 laid the posts out in paired rows — one grid row per post, card on alternating sides. A three-line text post sitting opposite a tall image post left its whole row half empty, and the taller the image, the bigger the hole. That is where the emptiness came from; it was structural, not a spacing problem.

V2 drops rows entirely. The two columns are independent stacks, so a short post never reserves height on the opposite side. Getting them to actually finish level took two more passes than expected, and the numbers are worth seeing because they are the difference between “designed” and “ragged”:

Deal in date order, always to the shorter column — the obvious approach — finished 1054px apart. Whichever column receives the last tall card overshoots, and a text post is 200px against an image post's 740px.
Walking trailing cards across to close the gap got it to 474px. Better, still a visible ragged tail.
Largest-first packing — place the tall cards while there is still slack to absorb them, fill the rest with the small ones, then re-sort each column back into date order — finishes 20px apart. Sorting does not change heights, so the balance survives it and each column still reads newest-first.

That last number is the one that matters: 20px on a 4100px column, and it holds at any post count, because the packing is recomputed from measured heights every time.

The vertebrae are then placed after layout, at each card's measured midpoint, with filler vertebrae between them so the spine reads as one continuous column rather than a dotted line. A connector reaches from each anchored vertebra out to its card. All of it is re-measured on resize.

The charge effect is scroll-linked, not a loop: the lit section of the rail tracks how far down the column you are, so the signal appears to travel down the spine as you read. Vertebrae light as the charge front passes them.

Where this still costs you

Mobile is still a compromise, and it always will be. Below 900px there is no room for two columns either side of a spine, so the rail moves to the left edge and the posts stack in one column beside it. That reads well — it becomes a timeline rather than a spine — but the alternating symmetry, the thing that makes this option distinctive, is a desktop reward. Worth knowing given where most music traffic comes from.

It needs content to look right. Below about ten posts the columns are too short for the spine to feel like a column, and the filler vertebrae end up outnumbering the real ones. Fourteen are shown here. That is the honest threshold for rotating this in.

The measuring pass is real work. Vertebra placement and connectors depend on measured card positions, so they run after layout and again on resize, and they have to wait for images to decode or every anchor lands in the wrong place. That is handled here, but it is the part that makes this option more expensive than the Signal Log rather than just prettier.

On the X-ray treatment: its glow uses --color-moonlight, which the token file reserves for imagery and forbids in UI chrome. A spine graphic is arguably imagery rather than chrome, but it is a judgement call, and it is the one place any of this bends a stated rule. The geometric and circuit treatments stay strictly inside bone/gray.
