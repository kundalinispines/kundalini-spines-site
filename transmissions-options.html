Transmissions
as an update channel

Five ways the page could stop being a gallery of one numbered piece and start being the place your Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube activity lands. Every mockup below is live — the filters, tabs and hovers all work — and every one uses the real palette, real type and real cover art.

Read this before you pick

The hard part of this page is not the design. It is that none of these platforms will hand you a free, automatic feed anymore. That single fact shapes which option is cheap and which is expensive, so it is worth thirty seconds:

PlatformAuto-feed?What it actually takes
X / TwitterNoTimeline API is paid tier only. Single posts can still be embedded one at a time.
InstagramNoNeeds a Meta app, review, and a long-lived token you re-fresh. Weeks of setup, breaks on policy changes.
TikTokPartlyIndividual videos embed cleanly. A profile feed needs their Display API and approval.
YouTubeYesGenuinely easy — a channel RSS feed, no key, no approval.
SpotifyYesEmbeddable player per release, no key.

So there are three honest ways to fill any of these designs:

A — You post it here too. A line added to data/transmissions.json per update. No API, no keys, nothing to break, and it works the day it ships. Costs you about a minute per post.

B — A paid aggregator (Curator.io, Behold, EmbedSocial). Roughly $15–30/month, handles the tokens, but their markup lands inside your page and it will not look like the mockups below without a fight.

C — Hybrid. YouTube and Spotify pull themselves in for free; X, Instagram and TikTok get added by hand. Most of the benefit, none of the monthly fee.

Every option below works with A on day one. Where an option leans on something harder, its spec card says so.

Option 01

The Signal Log

One vertical column, newest first. Every update — a tweet, a TikTok, a numbered Transmission — is an entry in the same running log, stamped and sequenced.

This is the option that changes the page least and respects it most. Transmissions are already “numbered and never reused”; this keeps that idea and simply widens what counts as one. A tweet becomes 049. The archive framing survives, and the page reads as a record rather than a social wall.

Feels like
A dispatch log. Deliberate, archival, unhurried.
Best if
You post rarely and want each one to carry weight.
Content
Route A works fully. Nothing else needed.
Build
Lightest of the five. One template, one JSON file.
Weakness
Slow to scan once there are 60+ entries. Needs the filters to stay usable.

Option 02

Frequency Board

A dense mosaic of tiles at mixed sizes — a contact sheet. Image posts dominate, text posts become pull-quotes, and the biggest tile is whatever matters most this week.

Your artwork is the strongest asset the project has, and a single-column log shows one piece at a time. This shows twelve at once. It is the only option here that makes the page feel busy in a good way, and the only one that survives posting several times a day.

Feels like
A contact sheet, or a wall of evidence.
Best if
You post often and it is mostly visual.
Content
Route A works, but it is hungry — a thin board looks broken.
Build
Medium, with one real trap — see below.
Weakness
Text-only posts fight for space, and the platform edge-colours below break the “crimson only, never moonlight in UI” palette rule. Shown so you can judge it — easy to strip back to bone/gray.

The trap, found while building this: mixed tile sizes leave dead cells unless the spans happen to fill the rows. The first pass here sat at 84% fill — six visible holes, which read as a broken page rather than a designed one. Two fixes got it to 99%: grid-auto-flow: dense to backfill, and a rule that the tile spans in view must total a multiple of the column count. That is a real ongoing constraint on this option — every new post has to be assigned a size that keeps the board whole. The other four options have no equivalent rule.

Option 03

Channel Terminal

Each platform is a channel you tune to. The content area is a monospace readout — timestamped rows, scanlines, a blinking caret — and switching channels re-filters the feed in place.

The most on-concept of the five by a distance. “Transmissions”, “the Signal”, coded messages, numbered dispatches — the whole project already speaks in radio language, and this is the only option that takes that literally. It also degrades beautifully: an empty channel reads as NO SIGNAL rather than as a broken page.

Feels like
Tuning a shortwave set. Intercepted traffic.
Best if
You want the page to be a piece of the world, not a widget.
Content
Route A works. Handles thin channels better than any other option.
Build
Medium. The character is in the details — don't cheap out on them.
Weakness
Text-forward, so your artwork plays a supporting role. Strongest paired with a preview pane if imagery matters.

Option 04

Broadcast + Ledger

Split the page. The newest update takes the whole left side at full size; everything before it becomes a tight scrolling ledger on the right.

The only option built around recency. Someone landing here sees one thing first and knows immediately it is the current signal. That matters most around a release — drop day, a video premiere, an announcement — where you want the newest thing to be unmissable rather than merely first in a list.

Feels like
A station front page. One headline, then the wire.
Best if
Your updates cluster around releases and drops.
Content
Route A works. Rewards a good image or video on the newest post.
Build
Medium. The ledger needs its own scroll behaviour on desktop.
Weakness
Goes stale loudly. If the top post is three months old, the whole page announces it.

Option 05

The Spine

A single vertical column runs down the page. Each update is a vertebra on it — a small geometric node that lights as you scroll — with the post alternating left and right.

The only option that could not belong to any other band. Thirty-three vertebrae, seven nodes, one signal moving through the structure — that is the project's stated core idea, and this is the page where it becomes literal navigation. The most distinctive of the five, and the most expensive.

Feels like
An X-ray, or a spinal column read top to bottom.
Best if
You want one page that is unmistakably this project.
Content
Route A works. Best with 15–40 entries; awkward under 6.
Build
Heaviest. Custom SVG nodes, scroll-linked lighting, a real mobile rethink.
Weakness
Alternating layout collapses to one side on mobile, where most of your traffic will be — so the signature effect is mostly a desktop reward. It also burns vertical space: every post leaves the opposite column empty, so the page is roughly twice as long as Option 1 for the same content. Visible in the mockup below — judge it with that in mind.

If you want my read

Option 3, the Channel Terminal, is the one I'd build. It is the only design that comes out of the project's own vocabulary rather than being applied to it — the page is already called Transmissions, the concept is already a signal being received, and every other option would work just as well for any other artist. It also has the single most useful practical property of the five: it looks correct when a channel is empty. “NO SIGNAL ON THIS FREQUENCY” is on-brand. An empty mosaic is just a broken page.

Option 1, the Signal Log, is the safe pick, and it is genuinely good. It is the cheapest to build, the easiest to keep filled, and it preserves the numbered-and-never-reused idea you already established. If you want this live next week rather than next month, build this one.

Option 5, the Spine, is the one to want and be careful about. It is the most distinctive thing on this page and the only one nobody else could copy. It is also the most work, and its best trick — the alternating column — is exactly the part that has to be thrown away on phones. Worth doing if this page is going to be a destination. Not worth it if it is a place people glance at.

A note on all five: I would start on route A — posting updates into data/transmissions.json yourself — whichever design you choose. It costs a minute per post, it never breaks, and it means the page is real immediately instead of waiting on Meta's app review. Wiring YouTube's free RSS in afterwards is a small, separate job that does not change the design.
